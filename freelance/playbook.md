# Freelance ops playbook

Working reference for each session. Pipeline lives in `pipeline.csv` (dates as YYYY-MM-DD).

## 1. Scoring rubric (keep 7+)

Score each factor 1–10, then take the weighted total.

| Factor | Weight | 9–10 | 6–8 | 1–5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fit | 30% | Squarely in our list, deliverable end-to-end without the owner | Deliverable, one unknown to confirm | Needs calls, identity, physical work, or unverifiable credentials |
| Budget | 20% | $150+ per hour of owner review time | $60–150 | Under $60 |
| Client quality | 20% | Payment verified, 70%+ hire rate, $5k+ spent, 4.8+ reviews | Verified, some history | Unverified, no hires, or poor reviews |
| Scope clarity | 15% | Clear deliverable, format, and "done" | One or two gaps a question can close | Vague, open-ended, or shifting |
| Competition | 15% | Under 10 proposals, posted under 6h ago | 10–20 proposals | 50+ proposals or 48h+ old |

Automatic skip regardless of score: phone or video calls required, UGC or voice as a real person, academic work for a student, fake reviews, off-platform payment or contact requested, "test task" that is the whole job unpaid.

Flag to owner before bidding: anything where hitting the quality bar is uncertain (unfamiliar stack, regulated topic such as medical, legal or financial advice, very large data volume).

## 2. Proposal rules

- Under 150 words. First line names their specific problem in their words.
- One concrete mini-sample or plan from their actual brief, never a generic one.
- Fixed price and delivery date. One smart question.
- AI line where the platform or client requires disclosure: "I work with AI-assisted workflows and review every deliverable by hand before it reaches you."
- No invented track record. Cite only past work the owner has confirmed.
- Owner approves and sends. Nothing is submitted on their behalf.

## 3. Proposal skeletons

Replace every `[bracket]` from the job post. Delete any line that would be generic.

### SEO article / blog post

> You need [N] articles on [topic] that rank for [keyword] and sound like [brand], not filler.
>
> Three headline options for the first piece:
> 1. [headline targeting main keyword]
> 2. [headline with a number or year]
> 3. [headline answering the searcher's question]
>
> Each article comes with an outline for your sign-off, sources linked, meta title and description, and internal link suggestions.
>
> [N] articles, [word count] each: $[price], first draft in [X] days. [AI line]
>
> Quick question: who is the reader — someone comparing options, or someone ready to buy?

### Website / landing page copy

> Your [page] has to make [audience] understand [offer] in a few seconds, and right now [specific issue from their site or brief].
>
> Here's how I'd open the hero:
> **[Headline: outcome for the customer]**
> [One-line subhead: how, for whom]
> [Button text]
>
> You get [pages/sections], two headline variants per page, and copy laid out section by section so your designer can drop it in.
>
> $[price], delivered in [X] days, one revision round included. [AI line]
>
> What's the one action you want a visitor to take?

### Email sequence / newsletter

> You want [goal: e.g. more first purchases] from [list segment], and a [N]-email sequence is the right tool.
>
> Proposed flow:
> 1. [Email 1 purpose] — subject: "[subject line]"
> 2. [Email 2 purpose] — subject: "[subject line]"
> 3. [Email 3 purpose] — subject: "[subject line]"
>
> Each email includes two subject lines, preview text, and a single clear call to action, formatted for [their email tool].
>
> $[price] for [N] emails, delivered in [X] days. [AI line]
>
> What's the current open and click rate, so I know what we're beating?

### Excel / Google Sheets model or dashboard

> You need [report/dashboard] that turns [data source] into [the decision they make] without manual copy-paste each [week/month].
>
> My approach:
> - Raw data tab you paste into, untouched by formulas
> - Calculation tab with [key metrics from brief]
> - Dashboard tab: [chart 1], [chart 2], filters for [dimension]
>
> Everything is formula-driven, documented, and tested against a sample of your numbers before handoff.
>
> $[price], delivered in [X] days with a short how-to note. [AI line]
>
> Can you share a sample export (dummy values are fine) so I match your column layout exactly?

### Data cleaning / analysis / research report

> You have [dataset/question] and need [clean file / clear answer] you can trust, not just a tidier mess.
>
> Plan:
> 1. Profile the data: duplicates, blanks, inconsistent [fields]
> 2. Clean with documented rules, so every change is traceable
> 3. Deliver [cleaned file / findings report] plus a log of what changed and why
>
> For research, every claim is sourced with a link.
>
> $[price], delivered in [X] days. [AI line]
>
> Roughly how many rows, and which field matters most to get right?

### Landing page / website build

> You need a [type] page for [product] that loads fast, works on mobile, and gets [audience] to [action].
>
> What I'd build:
> - [Section list from brief: hero, proof, features, FAQ, form]
> - [Stack: e.g. HTML/CSS/JS or their platform], responsive, form connected to [tool]
> - Tested on mobile and desktop browsers before handoff
>
> $[price], live preview in [X] days, one revision round. [AI line]
>
> Do you have copy and brand assets ready, or should I include copy in the scope?

### Script / automation / API integration / bug fix

> [Their process] is costing you [time/errors], and it can run on its own: [trigger] → [action] → [result].
>
> Approach:
> 1. Confirm inputs, outputs, and edge cases with you
> 2. Build in [language/tool] with error handling and logging
> 3. Test against real examples and send you the results before handoff
>
> You get the code, setup steps, and a plain-language note on how it works.
>
> $[price], delivered in [X] days. [AI line]
>
> What should happen when [most likely failure: e.g. the API is down / a field is empty]?

### Pitch deck / business document

> You're [raising / pitching / applying] and need a [N]-slide deck that makes [audience] see [core point] quickly.
>
> Suggested flow: [Problem] → [Solution] → [Market] → [Traction] → [Model] → [Team] → [Ask]
>
> I write the narrative and slide copy, structure the numbers, and lay it out cleanly in [PowerPoint/Google Slides] using your branding.
>
> $[price], first draft in [X] days, one revision round. [AI line]
>
> What's the one objection you expect, so the deck answers it before it's asked?

### SEO audit / competitor research

> You want to know why [site] isn't ranking for [terms] and what to fix first.
>
> The audit covers:
> - Technical: indexing, speed, mobile, broken links
> - On-page: titles, headings, content gaps for [keywords]
> - Competitors: what [2–3 competitors] rank for that you don't
>
> You get a prioritized fix list (impact vs effort), not a 60-page export.
>
> $[price], delivered in [X] days. [AI line]
>
> Do you have Search Console access to share, or should I work from public data only?

## 4. After the win

### Scope restatement (send before starting)

> Thanks for hiring me. To confirm, I'm delivering: [deliverable, format, quantity]. Includes: [items]. Not included: [items]. Due: [date]. I need from you: [inputs]. If anything here is off, tell me now and I'll adjust before starting.

### Delivery note

> Attached: [files]. What I did: [2–3 lines]. How to use it: [steps]. Checked against your brief: [each requirement, ticked]. One free revision round is included — send me anything you'd like changed.

### Check-in (2–3 days after delivery)

> Hi [name], checking that [deliverable] is working for you. Anything you'd like adjusted? The revision round is still open.

### Review request and follow-on (only once they've confirmed they're happy)

> Glad it landed well. If you have a minute, a review would mean a lot. Also, since [observation from the job], a natural next step would be [specific follow-on]. Happy to scope it if useful.

## 5. Self-check before every handoff

- Every requirement in the brief is met and listed in the delivery note
- Writing: original, fact-checked, sources linked, client's tone and word count
- Data: formulas checked against a manual sample, no hard-coded numbers hiding in formulas
- Code: run and tested on real inputs, setup steps verified from scratch
- Delivered ahead of the due date
